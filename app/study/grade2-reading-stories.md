# Grade 2 — Reading Stories, Fables & Poems

## The parts of every story

| Part | What it is | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Characters** | who the story is about | Who? |
| **Setting** | where AND when it happens | Where? When? |
| **Problem** | the trouble | What went wrong? |
| **Solution** | how it gets fixed | How was it solved? |

The **beginning** introduces the characters, setting, and problem. The
**ending** wraps up (concludes) the action.

## Watch the characters

Second-grade readers watch **how characters respond** to big events:

- What do they **do**?
- How do they **feel**? (Feelings usually **change** — sad → happy,
  nervous → proud.)

You figure out feelings from what characters **do and say**: if Mia
"smiled and laughed," you know she's happy even if the story never says so.

## Fables and their morals

A **fable** is a short story where **animals talk and act like people** —
and it always teaches a lesson called the **moral**. Know these famous ones:

| Fable | Moral |
|---|---|
| The Tortoise and the Hare | Slow and steady wins the race. |
| The Boy Who Cried Wolf | If you lie, no one believes you when you tell the truth. |
| The Ant and the Grasshopper | Work hard and prepare for the future. |
| The Lion and the Mouse | Even small friends can be a big help. |
| The Crow and the Pitcher | Think your way out — little by little works. |
| The Wind and the Sun | Gentleness works better than force. |

Cousins of the fable: a **folktale** is passed down by telling (nobody knows
the author); a **fairy tale** has magic ("Once upon a time..."); a **tall
tale** stretches the truth to be funny.

## Points of view

Two characters can feel **totally differently** about the same thing — rain
ruins Sam's soccer game but gives Ana a whole day to paint. The words a
character says out loud sit inside **quotation marks**, and tags like *said,
shouted, whispered* tell you who's talking and how.

## Same story, different versions

The same story gets told around the world: France's *Cinderella* has a glass
slipper; China's *Yeh-Shen* has magic fish bones. Compare them:

- **Same:** a kind girl, magical help, a happy ending
- **Different:** names, places, and the magic details

Signal words: **both / same / also** mean alike; **but / different / unlike**
mean not alike.

## Poem words

- **Rhyme** — same ending sound: cat / hat, night / light
- **Rhythm** — the beat you can clap to
- **Repetition** — a line said again and again ("Run, run, as fast as you can!")
- **Alliteration** — same starting sound: "**S**illy **S**ally **s**ang **s**even **s**ongs"
- **Onomatopoeia** — a word that IS its sound: buzz, pop, crash
- **Stanza** — a group of lines, like a paragraph for poems

Poets pick these on purpose — they give a poem its music and its feeling.
