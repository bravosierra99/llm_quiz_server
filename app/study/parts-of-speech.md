# Parts of Speech — Every Word Has a Job

Think of a sentence like a team. Every word on the team has a **job**.
Some words *name* things. Some words show *action*. Some words *describe*.

**"Parts of speech" just means the different jobs a word can do.**

Here is the whole team at a glance:

| Job (part of speech) | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun** | Names a person, place, animal, or thing | dog, park, Jessica, cookie |
| **Verb** | Shows an action — what someone *does* | run, jump, sing, eat |
| **Adjective** | Describes a noun — what it's *like* | big, red, soft, happy |
| **Pronoun** | Takes the place of a noun | he, she, it, we, they |
| **Joining word** (conjunction) | Glues two ideas together | and, but, or, so, because |
| **Position word** (preposition) | Tells *where* something is | in, on, under, over, by |

The same word always keeps the sentence working — take a word out, and its job
doesn't get done!

## Nouns — the naming words

A **noun** names a **person, place, animal, or thing**.

> The **girl** ate a **cookie** at the **park** with her **dog**.

Every bold word is a noun — you could point to each one, or draw a picture of it.

**Spot-it trick:** if you can put *"a"* or *"the"* in front of it (a cookie, the
park), it's probably a noun.

## Verbs — the action words

A **verb** is what someone **does**: run, jump, sing, swim, eat, read.

> The bird **sings** a song.

"Sings" is the action the bird does — that's the verb.

**Spot-it trick:** can you *do* it? You can jump. You can eat. You can't "chair"
or "green" — those aren't actions, so they aren't verbs.

### Verbs also tell WHEN

Verbs change a little to show **when** the action happens:

| When? | How it looks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** — already happened | often ends in **-ed** | She **walked** to school. |
| **Now** (present) | plain, or ends in -s | She **walks** to school. |
| **Future** — hasn't happened yet | uses **will** | She **will walk** to school. |

**Spot-it tricks:** see **-ed** on the end? It already happened. See **will**?
It's coming later.

## Adjectives — the describing words

An **adjective** describes a noun. It tells more about it — the **size, color,
shape, or feeling**.

> The **tall** man waved. The **red** ball bounced. The **soft** pillow. A **happy** baby.

**Spot-it trick:** an adjective answers *"what kind?"* What kind of ball? A
**red** ball. What kind of man? A **tall** man.

## Pronouns — the stand-in words

A **pronoun** takes the place of a noun, so you don't say the same name over and
over.

Instead of: *Sara is nice. **Sara** is my friend.*
Say: *Sara is nice. **She** is my friend.*

| Who you're talking about | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| A boy or man | **he** |
| A girl or woman | **she** |
| An animal or thing | **it** |
| You + someone else ("Tom and I") | **we** |
| Two or more people | **they** |
| Yourself | **I**, **me** |

**Spot-it trick:** a pronoun only works if you already know who it means. "**She**
is my friend" only makes sense after you've said *Sara*.

## Joining words — the glue (conjunctions)

A **joining word** glues two ideas into one sentence: **and, but, or, so,
because**. The fancy name is *conjunction*.

Each glue word joins in its own way:

| Glue word | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | adds one more | I like dogs **and** cats. |
| **but** | shows a surprise or change | I ran fast, **but** I missed the bus. |
| **or** | gives a choice | Do you want milk **or** juice? |
| **so** | shows what happened next (the result) | I was tired, **so** I went to bed. |
| **because** | gives the reason why | I went to bed **because** I was tired. |

Notice **so** and **because** are opposites in direction: *tired → **so** → bed*,
but *bed ← **because** ← tired*.

## Position words — the where words (prepositions)

A **position word** tells **where** something is: **in, on, under, over, by**.
The fancy name is *preposition*.

> The cat is **in** the box. The book is **on** the table. The ball rolled
> **under** the bed.

**Spot-it trick:** think of a cat and a box. Anywhere the cat can be — **in**
the box, **on** the box, **under** the box, **by** the box — that word is a
position word.

## Try it! One sentence, every job

> **The** **little** **dog** **ran** **under** **the** **table**, **and** **it** **barked**.

| Word | Its job |
|---|---|
| little | adjective (what kind of dog? little) |
| dog | noun (names an animal) |
| ran | verb (the action — past tense!) |
| under | position word (tells where) |
| table | noun (names a thing) |
| and | joining word (glues the two ideas) |
| it | pronoun (stands in for "the dog") |
| barked | verb (another action — it ends in -ed, so it already happened) |
