# Grade 2 — Grammar (Plurals, Pronouns, Past Tense & Sentences)

## Collective nouns — one word for a whole group

English has special words for groups:

> a **team** of players · a **class** of students · a **crowd** of people ·
> a **herd** of cows · a **flock** of birds · a **pack** of wolves ·
> a **swarm** of bees · a **school** of fish · a **litter** of puppies ·
> a **pride** of lions

Yes — fish swim in a *school*!

## Irregular plurals — words that break the -s rule

Most nouns add -s or -es to mean more than one. These rebels **change
instead** (you just have to know them):

| One | More than one | One | More than one |
|---|---|---|---|
| foot | **feet** | mouse | **mice** |
| tooth | **teeth** | goose | **geese** |
| child | **children** | person | **people** |
| man | **men** | leaf | **leaves** |
| woman | **women** | wolf | **wolves** |

And some don't change at all: one **fish**, two **fish** — same for
**sheep**, **deer**, and **moose**.

## Reflexive pronouns — the "-self" words

When someone does something **to or for themselves**, the pronoun points
back at them:

> I made it **myself**. · You can do it **yourself**. · She built it
> **herself**. · We did it **ourselves**. · They cleaned up **themselves**.

The full team: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves,
yourselves, themselves. **Warning:** "hisself" and "theirselves" are NOT
real words — it's *himself* and *themselves*.

## Irregular past tense — verbs that time-travel their own way

Most verbs add **-ed** to tell about the past. These change the whole word:

> sit → **sat** · go → **went** · see → **saw** · eat → **ate** ·
> run → **ran** · tell → **told** · hide → **hid** · make → **made** ·
> write → **wrote** · fly → **flew** · grow → **grew** · know → **knew** ·
> catch → **caught** · teach → **taught** · think → **thought** ·
> buy → **bought** · sleep → **slept** · break → **broke**

Never "runned," "goed," or "catched"!

## Adjective or adverb?

- An **adjective** describes a **thing** (a noun): "the **quick** dog"
- An **adverb** describes an **action** (a verb) — how, when, or where:
  "the dog ran **quickly**" — most adverbs end in **-ly**

**Spot-it trick:** describing the *dog*? Adjective. Describing the
*running*? Adverb. And it's always "He sings **well**" — never "he sings
good."

## Simple and compound sentences

A **complete sentence** = a **naming part** (who or what) + a **telling
part** (what they do). "Ran to the store." is missing its who — that's a
**fragment**, not a sentence.

A **compound sentence** glues TWO complete thoughts together with a
**comma + joining word** — and, but, or, so:

> I like pizza**, and** my sister likes tacos.
> It rained**, so** we stayed inside.

You can also **expand** a sentence by adding details: "The dog barked." →
"The **big brown** dog barked **loudly at the mail truck**."
