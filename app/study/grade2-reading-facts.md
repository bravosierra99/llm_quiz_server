# Grade 2 — Reading for Facts (Informational Text)

Some books tell stories. Others teach you **true facts** — about bees,
volcanoes, or the Chesapeake Bay. Fact books have their own tools, and good
readers know how to use every one.

## What is it mostly about?

- The **main topic** = what the **whole text** is mostly about.
- Each **paragraph** has its own **focus** — what that one paragraph is about.
- **Key details** are the facts that tell you more.

**Spot-it trick:** ask *"What is this MOSTLY about?"* A word that shows up
again and again is a big clue.

## Text features — the book's toolbox

| Feature | Where | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Table of contents** | front | chapters in order, with page numbers |
| **Heading** | top of a section | tells what that section is about |
| **Bold print** | in the text | flags an important word |
| **Caption** | under a picture | explains what the picture shows |
| **Diagram** | in the text | labeled drawing — parts of something, or how it works |
| **Glossary** | back | A-to-Z list of words **with their meanings** |
| **Index** | back | A-to-Z list of topics **with page numbers** |

**The classic mix-up:** glossary and index are both alphabetical and both at
the back. Remember: **G**lossary **G**ives meanings; **I**ndex says where to
f**I**nd it.

## Why did the author write it?

Three purposes — remember **PIE**:

- **P**ersuade — get you to think or do something ("Everyone should wear a helmet!")
- **I**nform — teach you facts (a book about bees)
- **E**ntertain — tell an enjoyable story

Authors also make **points** and back them up with **reasons** — listen for
**because**.

## Order, causes, and effects

- **Sequence words** — *first, next, then, finally* — tell you the order of
  steps: egg → caterpillar → chrysalis → butterfly.
- **Cause and effect** — the **cause** is *why* it happened; the **effect**
  is *what* happened. "**Because** it rained, the game was canceled." Cause:
  rain. Effect: no game. Signal words: because, so, since.

## Fact or opinion?

- A **fact** can be **proven true**: "A spider has eight legs."
- An **opinion** is what someone **thinks or feels**: "Spiders are creepy."

Opinion clue words: *think, feel, believe, best, worst, favorite, should.*
