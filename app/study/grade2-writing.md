# Grade 2 — The Three Kinds of Writing

In second grade you write three different kinds of pieces. Each one has its
own recipe.

## 1. Opinion writing — what you THINK, and why

**Recipe:**
1. Introduce your topic
2. State your opinion: *"I think..."* / *"The best... is..."*
3. Give **reasons**
4. Glue them together with linking words: **because, and, also**
5. Finish with a concluding statement: *"That is why..."*

> I think dogs are the best pets. One reason is **because** they are loyal.
> **Also**, dogs love to play. **And** they can learn tricks. **That is
> why** dogs are the best pets.

## 2. Informative writing — TEACH the reader

**Recipe:**
1. Introduce your topic
2. Teach with **facts** (things that can be proven) and **definitions**
   (what words mean)
3. Finish with a concluding sentence

> Honeybees are amazing insects. A honeybee collects nectar, which is a
> sweet liquid inside flowers. Bees turn the nectar into honey. Honeybees
> are small, but they do big work.

No opinions allowed — just true things!

## 3. Narrative writing — tell what HAPPENED

**Recipe:**
1. Tell the events **in order**
2. Add details about your **actions, thoughts, and feelings**
3. Use **time-order words**: first, next, then, after, later, finally
4. End with **closure** — wrap it up (how it turned out, how you felt)

> **One morning**, we drove to the beach. **First** I built a giant sand
> castle. **Then** a big wave crashed over it! I thought all my work was
> ruined, and I felt sad. **But then** my dad helped me build an even
> bigger one. It was the best beach day ever.

## Which kind does the prompt want?

| The prompt says... | Write a(n)... |
|---|---|
| "What do you **think**...? Which is **best**?" | opinion piece |
| "**Teach** the reader about..." / "Explain..." | informative piece |
| "**Tell about a time** when..." | narrative |

## Making it better: revise, then edit

- **Revising** = making the writing BETTER: add details, change boring
  words to strong ones, put things in a better order
- **Editing** = fixing MISTAKES: capitals, punctuation, spelling

First make it good, then make it correct!
